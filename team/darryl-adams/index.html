About

Close the gap between what technology can do and what people need it to do.

Darryl founded Access Insights after nearly 28 years at Intel. His career there began in enterprise technology, managing software development and implementation programs for global customer support and warranty operations, then grew through digital health, product development, and emerging technology programs spanning software, firmware, and hardware. Along the way he built document lifecycle and compliance systems for Intel Digital Health, defined scope and resources for the Intel Reader, and managed programs exploring wireless charging and blockchain applications.

As Intel's Director of Accessibility from 2021 to 2024, he led the company's Accessibility Office and global accessibility strategy, connecting accessible product experiences with disability inclusion in the workplace. He helped establish the Accessible Technology Program and the Accessibility Champions Network, and chaired Intel's Disability Leadership Council.

Darryl lives with progressive vision loss and single-sided deafness, and his interest in accessibility grew from adapting to those changes while continuing a demanding technology career. That experience informs his emphasis on involving people with disabilities throughout research and development, and on treating user confidence and autonomy as essential outcomes. Technology alone cannot remove every barrier: in written testimony on Oregon's Senate Bill 20 in 2025, he advocated for employment opportunities that let people with disabilities advance their careers while keeping access to essential care and support.

Selected work

Technology that earns independence.

  • Indoor Wayfinding

    Intel · GoodMaps collaboration

    Research with Intel employees identified navigating unfamiliar buildings as a recurring barrier. Darryl helped advance Intel's collaboration with GoodMaps, and discussed and demonstrated the work publicly as it expanded to Portland State University, Portland International Airport, and facilities associated with the Paris 2024 Games.

  • Spatial Awareness Wearables

    Intel

    Contributed testing, advocacy, and outreach for Intel's spatial awareness wearable prototypes, demonstrating a RealSense based prototype during Intel's CES 2015 keynote.

  • Intel Reader

    Intel

    Defined scope and resources for the Intel Reader, a device that made printed information available through speech and magnification.

  • Gamers with Disabilities

    Intel · with anthropologist Jamie Sherman

    Explored the experiences of competitive gamers with disabilities as part of a yearlong study of gamers' lives and habits, connecting everyday barriers and community research to product decisions.

  • Hearing Technology Patents

    Intel

    Named co-inventor on four issued U.S. patents covering accessible computing interfaces and personalized hearing technologies, issued between 2024 and 2026.
